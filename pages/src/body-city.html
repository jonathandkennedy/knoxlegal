Florida probate, in plain English

Every estate is different, but nearly every {{CITY}} probate case runs through one of two doors at {{COURT_LINE}}.

Summary administration

The shorter path — generally available when the estate's non-exempt assets are $75,000 or less, or the death was more than two years ago. Often wrapped up in a matter of weeks.

Formal administration

The standard path for larger estates. The court appoints a personal representative and issues letters of administration, which give you legal authority over accounts and property. Typically several months to a year.

Creditors & deadlines

Florida gives creditors a 3-month window to file claims after notice is published, and known creditors must be served. Handled correctly, this cuts off late claims against the family.

An attorney is usually required

In most Florida formal administrations, the personal representative must be represented by an attorney. It's not just a good idea — it's how the court expects the estate to move.

Probate questions families ask us

Short answers here — specific answers for your estate in your free case review.

Do we actually have to go through probate?
If your loved one owned assets in their own name alone — a house, condo, bank or brokerage accounts, a car — probate is usually how those assets legally pass to the family. Assets with beneficiary designations or held jointly often pass outside probate. In your case review we'll tell you honestly if you don't need us.
How long does Florida probate take?
Summary administration is often finished in a matter of weeks. Formal administration typically runs six months to a year, driven mostly by the mandatory creditor period and the estate's complexity. We keep it moving and keep you updated.
What does it cost — and who pays?
You'll get a clear quote before we file. In most Florida probate cases, attorney's fees and court costs are paid from the estate's assets rather than out of the family's pocket. We'll explain exactly how that works for your estate.
I live out of state. Do I have to come to Florida?
Rarely. Filings are electronic and most hearings, when needed at all, can be attended remotely. Florida does limit who may serve as a personal representative from out of state — generally close family members qualify — and we'll confirm your eligibility in the case review.
There's no will. What happens now?
The estate still goes through probate — Florida's intestacy statutes decide who inherits (spouse and children first, then other relatives), and the court appoints a personal representative. Dying without a will does not mean the state takes the property; it means the court needs the process handled correctly.
The bank says it needs "letters of administration." Is that you?
Yes. Letters of administration are the court document proving your authority to act for the estate. Getting them issued is one of the first things we do when we open the administration.
