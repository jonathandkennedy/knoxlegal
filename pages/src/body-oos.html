Why Florida probate reaches so many families elsewhere

Florida real estate passes through Florida courts — no matter where the owner lived or where the heirs live now.

The snowbird estate

Your parent wintered here or retired here. Their Florida home, condo, and accounts go through Florida probate, even if the family is in New York, New Jersey, or Ohio.

Ancillary probate

The estate is being probated in another state, but there's Florida property. Florida requires its own proceeding — ancillary administration — and we run it alongside your home-state counsel.

International heirs

Heirs abroad, foreign owners of Florida property — we coordinate documents, translations, and distributions across borders.

How remote probate actually works

Everything is electronic

Florida courts e-file. Petitions, inventories, accountings — none of it requires your presence, and hearings, when needed at all, are typically attended remotely.

Signatures come to you

Documents are signed where you live — with notarization arranged near you — and the estate's mail, property, and sale logistics are managed from our end.

You stay informed, not involved

Regular updates in plain English. You make the decisions that matter; we do everything else.

One thing to check early: Florida limits who may serve as a personal representative from out of state — generally close family members qualify. We confirm your eligibility in the first conversation.

Out-of-state questions

Do I ever have to appear in a Florida courtroom?
Almost never. Routine probate matters are decided on the filings, and the rare hearing can usually be attended by phone or video. In nearly all of our out-of-state matters, the client never travels to Florida.
Can you sell the Florida property for us?
Yes — we obtain the court authority to sell, coordinate with a local realtor and the title company, resolve any title defects, and close the sale with proceeds flowing to the estate. You sign from home.
We already have a probate lawyer in our home state. Do we still need you?
For Florida property, yes — the home-state proceeding can't transfer Florida real estate. We handle the Florida ancillary administration and work directly with your home-state counsel so the two cases move together.
How do we get started from another state?
The same free case review as everyone else — by phone or video. Have the death certificate and a rough list of Florida assets if you can; if you can't, we'll help you find out what exists.
