Two doors into Florida probate

Summary administration

For estates with $75,000 or less in non-exempt assets, or when the death was more than two years ago. Faster and lighter — often finished in weeks.

Formal administration

The standard path for larger estates. The court appoints a personal representative and issues letters of administration — the document every bank and title company asks for. Typically several months to a year.

With or without a will

No will doesn't mean no probate — it means Florida's intestacy statutes decide who inherits, and the process matters even more. We handle both.

The complex ones

Multiple properties, businesses, title defects, missing heirs, assets in several states. This is the work Rachel is known for — see Estate Disputes when it turns adversarial.

Administration questions

I was named personal representative. What am I actually signing up for?
You become a fiduciary: responsible for gathering assets, notifying creditors, filing on time, and treating every beneficiary fairly — with personal liability if it's done wrong. With counsel handling the mechanics, the role is very manageable; that's exactly what we do for personal representatives.
Do I need an attorney for this?
In most Florida formal administrations, yes — the rules require the personal representative to be represented by an attorney, with narrow exceptions. Even in summary administration, one properly handled filing is usually cheaper than one mistake.
Who pays for probate?
In most Florida probate cases, attorney's fees and court costs are paid from estate assets rather than out of the family's pocket — and you'll have a clear quote before we file anything.
The estate has a house with a title problem. Can you fix that?
Yes — resolving title defects through probate is one of Rachel's signatures. A property that can't be sold because of an old deed issue is exactly the kind of estate we take on.
