What Florida owes you as a beneficiary

Personal representatives and trustees are fiduciaries. That word has teeth.

Information

  • Notice that the estate was opened, and a copy of the will
  • An inventory of what the estate actually holds
  • Accountings that show every dollar in and out

Action

  • Distributions made without unreasonable delay
  • Estate property protected, insured, and not "borrowed"
  • A fiduciary who can be surcharged or removed when they breach

Beneficiary questions

Will this tear the family apart?
Most beneficiary matters are resolved with firm letters and court-required disclosure, not scorched-earth litigation. Very often, transparency alone solves the problem — and when it doesn't, that tells you what you needed to know.
What does it cost me to enforce my rights?
You'll know the structure up front. Depending on the matter, fees may be payable from the estate or the recovery; we'll walk through what applies to your situation in the free review.
I live out of state — can you still represent me?
Yes. Most of our beneficiary clients are out of state. Everything — review, filings, hearings, updates — is handled remotely. See Out-of-State Families.
How long do I have?
Some rights expire on short statutory clocks — objections to a will can be barred three months after formal notice, sometimes sooner. If something feels wrong, get the deadline checked now, even if you're not ready to act.
