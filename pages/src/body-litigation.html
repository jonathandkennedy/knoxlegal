Why timing decides these cases

Short statutory windows

Once formal notice of administration is served, objections to a will can be barred in three months — and some objections far sooner. Waiting quietly forfeits rights.

Evidence goes cold

Bank records, medical charts, and witnesses to a signing are freshest early. The sooner we start, the more of the story we can prove.

Assets keep moving

While a family deliberates, property gets sold and accounts get drained. Courts can freeze estate assets — but only if someone asks.

Estate dispute questions

How do I know if a will can be challenged?
Florida wills are challenged on specific grounds — undue influence, lack of capacity, improper execution, fraud, or forgery. Red flags include last-minute changes favoring one person, isolation of the decedent, and a will that surfaced from the person who benefits. A case assessment tells you quickly whether the facts support a contest and what the deadline is in your case.
The executor won't tell us anything. Is that legal?
No. Personal representatives owe beneficiaries information — including a notice of administration, an inventory, and accountings. Silence is often the first sign of a deeper problem, and Florida courts will compel disclosure. See our Beneficiary Representation page.
What does estate litigation cost?
It depends on the dispute, and you'll know the structure before we start. In many matters, fees can be addressed from the estate or the recovery — we'll discuss what applies to your case honestly in the assessment.
Can this be resolved without a trial?
Usually, yes — most estate disputes settle once the facts are on the table, often through mediation. Preparing each case as if it will be tried is exactly what produces those settlements.
